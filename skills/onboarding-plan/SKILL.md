# Onboarding Plan — from "they said yes" to "they are doing the job"

Most new hires lose their first weeks to missing access, unclear
expectations and nobody to ask. This skill writes the plan that prevents it:
what must be ready before they arrive, whom they meet in the first days and
why, and what "on track" means at one week, one month and three months —
agreed with the manager, in the new hire's hands on day one.

## Related skills
- **job-description** — the outcomes at twelve months come from it; this
  plan is the first quarter of them.
- **action-tracker** — the plan's actions (access, meetings, check-ins) go
  into the tracker with owners and dates.
- **meeting-notes** — the check-ins are recorded with it.
(If a referenced skill is not installed, do the equivalent inline.)

## When to use
- A hire is confirmed and a start date exists.
- **Do NOT use** to write a generic company handbook; the plan is for one
  person in one role. Do not schedule anything in other people's calendars
  without the manager's yes.

## Workflow
1. **Ask the manager, once**: start date, role and the job description, who
   the person's first contact is, the five people they must meet in week
   one and why, the tools and accesses the role needs, one small real piece
   of work they can finish in the first week, and how the manager wants to
   check in (cadence).
2. **Before day one** — the checklist: accounts, devices, accesses, where
   the documents live, who sends the welcome, what they receive the day
   before. Each item with an owner and a date before the start.
3. **Week one** — a day-by-day: who they meet (name, role, what to learn
   from them), what they read, the small real piece of work, the end-of-week
   check-in.
4. **Month one and month three** — what they should have done and be able to
   do on their own, in the role's terms (from the job description's
   outcomes), and the check-in questions the manager asks at each point.
5. **Deliver** two versions under Output: the manager's (with owners and
   internal notes) and the new hire's (what to expect, whom to ask), and
   put the actions into `action-tracker` if it is installed.

## Standards
- Every access and tool is listed by name with the person who grants it; "IT
  will sort it" is not an item.
- The week-one piece of work is real and finishable — something used
  afterwards, not an exercise.
- Expectations at each milestone are outcomes, not attendance.
- The new hire's version has no internal notes about them.
- Check-ins are on the calendar before day one, not "when there is time".

## Output
- **Before day one** — checklist: item · owner · by when.
- **Week one** — day by day: meetings (who, why), reading, the first piece
  of work, Friday check-in.
- **By one month / by three months** — outcomes, and the check-in questions.
- **Who to ask** — first contact, manager, the go-to per topic.
Saved to `orch-files` as `hiring/<role>/onboarding-<name>.md`, two versions.

## Defaults
- Cadence unknown → daily ten minutes in week one, weekly after.
- Remote hire → the meetings are calls, and the first day includes one
  informal conversation with no agenda.
- No job description → build the milestones from what the manager says and
  mark them as provisional.
