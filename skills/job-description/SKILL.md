# Job Description — the role, said so the right person recognises it

A job description is a filter and a promise: it should make the right
candidate think "that is me" and the wrong one move on, and everything it
promises has to be true. This skill gets the real role out of the hiring
manager — what the person will actually do, what "good" looks like in a year,
what is truly required — and writes it plainly.

## Related skills
- **candidate-screening** — uses the must-haves from here as its criteria.
- **interview-guide** — the interview tests what this describes.
- **voice-guide** — if the company has a voice, the post is in it.
(If a referenced skill is not installed, do the equivalent inline.)

## When to use
- A role is opening, or an existing post is not bringing the right people.
- **Do NOT use** to list twenty requirements nobody has: every must-have
  costs candidates. Never include age, gender, nationality, family situation,
  health, or anything else that is not about doing the job; if the person
  asks for one, say why it is out and offer the job-related version.

## Workflow
1. **Ask the hiring manager, in one or two rounds**: what the person will do
   in a normal week; what they must have delivered after twelve months for
   the hire to have been right; who they work with and report to; the three
   things without which the hire cannot work (must-haves) and what would be
   nice; pay range, location, remote or not, hours; the process and how
   long it takes.
2. **Separate must from nice.** A must-have is something they cannot learn in
   the first months. Push back once on each must-have that is really a
   preference.
3. **Write the post** in the shape under Output: outcomes and real work
   before requirements, plain words, short, no jargon the candidate would
   not use about themselves.
4. **Check** against the Standards, then show it for the manager's yes.
   Deliver ready to paste, and a short version for a social post if they
   want one.

## Standards
- Title people search for, not an internal grade.
- The first paragraph says what the person will do and why it matters, not
  what the company is.
- Every requirement is job-related and phrased as a capability, not a
  credential, unless the credential is legally required.
- Pay range stated when the person allows it; it is the single most
  effective line in the post. Say so if they leave it out.
- The process is described honestly: steps, who they meet, how long.
- No claims about culture that the manager could not give an example of.

## Output
- **Title** · location · type · pay range.
- **What you will do** — 4–6 concrete lines, present tense.
- **What good looks like in a year** — 3 outcomes.
- **What you need** — the must-haves (3–5) · **Nice to have** (up to 3).
- **How we work** — team, reporting, one honest paragraph.
- **The process** — steps and timing.
- **How to apply** — what to send, to whom, by when.

## Defaults
- Language: the one candidates will read; ask if unclear.
- Pay range refused → post without it, and note the likely cost in
  applicants.
- Length: one page; a longer post is a longer list of requirements.
