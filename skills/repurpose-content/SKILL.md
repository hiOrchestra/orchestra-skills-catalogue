# Repurpose Content — one piece → the versions each channel needs

A good piece is read once where it lives and five more times elsewhere, if it
is reshaped for each place. This skill takes a finished piece and produces the
derivatives that make sense for the person's channels — each one written for
that channel's reader, length and habits, in the same voice, pointing back to
the original.

## Related skills
- **voice-guide** — read it first; a derivative in the wrong voice does more
  harm than no derivative.
- **write-article** — the original; this skill starts where it ends.
- **distill** — a faithful summary for someone to read; this skill writes
  pieces for someone to publish.
(If a referenced skill is not installed, do the equivalent inline.)

## When to use
- A piece is finished (published or approved) and the person wants reach.
- **Do NOT use** on a draft — derivatives of a piece that changes are wasted.
  Do not produce a channel the person does not use; ask which they have.

## Workflow
1. **Read the original whole**, and find its one idea: the sentence a reader
   would repeat to a colleague. Every derivative carries that sentence's idea,
   in that channel's words.
2. **Ask which channels**, once, and remember the answer (`orch-files`,
   `voice/channels.md`): newsletter, LinkedIn, X, Instagram caption, the site's
   home or category blurb, an internal note. Each channel gets the version its
   reader expects — length, opening, whether links are allowed, hashtags or not.
3. **Write each derivative from the idea, not from the paragraphs.** An excerpt
   with the middle cut out reads as one. A LinkedIn post has its own first
   line; a thread has one point per message; a newsletter blurb says why to
   read, not what it says.
4. **Point back.** Each derivative names or links the original, where the
   channel allows.
5. **Check voice and facts.** Same voice guide, same numbers, same names — a
   derivative is where numbers get rounded wrong.
6. **Deliver as a set** under Output, each marked with its channel, ready to
   paste. Publishing them is the person's act unless a channel skill is
   installed and they said to.

## Standards
- Nothing is claimed in a derivative that the original does not support.
- Each derivative stands on its own — a reader who never opens the original
  still gets one whole idea.
- Length fits the channel: a LinkedIn post that is the article's first 300
  words is not a LinkedIn post.
- No hashtags, emoji or hooks the person does not use themselves (check the
  voice guide).

## Output
One block per channel, in this order: channel name, the text ready to paste,
character count, the link or reference to the original, and a one-line note if
something was left out on purpose (a claim that needs context, a number that
needs the table).

## Defaults
- Channels unknown → ask once; if no answer, deliver newsletter blurb and
  LinkedIn post, and say the rest is available.
- One derivative per channel; a series (a thread, a week of posts) only if asked.
- Quotes: only sentences the original contains, verbatim, attributed.
